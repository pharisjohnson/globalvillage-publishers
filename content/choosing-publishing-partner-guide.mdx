# Choosing a Publishing Partner: 5 Critical Things to Look For

You have a story worth telling. A corporate milestone. An institutional legacy. A cultural treasure that deserves to be captured between covers. You have the vision, the archives, and the commitment.

Now comes one of the most consequential decisions of your project: *Who will bring it to life?*

Choosing a publishing partner is not like hiring a printer. A printer puts ink on paper. A publishing partner shapes the entire journey — from editorial direction and design through to printing, distribution, and legacy. The wrong choice can mean missed deadlines, disappointing quality, or a book that never quite lives up to the vision. The right choice produces a volume that exceeds expectations and endures for generations.

At Global Village Publishers E.A Ltd, we have been that partner for government ministries, multinational corporations, cultural institutions, and private individuals across East Africa. We have also seen what happens when a project is entrusted to someone who treats a commemorative book like a generic print job.

Here are the five things you must evaluate when choosing your publishing partner.

## 1. A Proven Track Record in Commemorative Publishing

Not all publishers are created equal, and a publisher who excels at textbooks or novels is not necessarily equipped for a premium coffee table book. Commemorative publishing is a distinct discipline. It requires:

- Experience handling archival and historical content
- Sensitivity to institutional and personal legacies
- The ability to manage multiple stakeholders
- Aesthetic judgment that matches the gravitas of the subject

**What to ask:** *Can you show us three to five completed commemorative or coffee table books? Can we speak with past clients?*

At GVP, our portfolio includes the *Best of Kenya* series, institutional histories for leading organisations, and commemorative volumes marking national milestones. We are proud of every one, and we are happy to put you in touch with clients who have been through the process with us.

**Red flag:** A portfolio dominated by basic brochures, text-heavy academic books, or generic print materials.

## 2. In-House Design and Editorial Capability

The best publishing partners bring creative firepower in-house, not outsourced to freelancers you will never meet. When design, editorial, and project management sit under one roof, communication is faster, quality control is tighter, and there is a single point of accountability.

**What to ask:** *Who will be our lead designer and our lead editor? Can we meet them? How many rounds of design revisions are included?*

GVP's Nairobi studio houses a dedicated team of book designers, editors, and production specialists. The people who attend your kickoff meeting are the same people who will craft your book. There are no handoffs to third parties, no communication gaps.

**Red flag:** The publisher cannot name the specific team members who will work on your project, or the design work is outsourced with the publisher acting only as a middleman.

## 3. Understanding of the East African Market

A commemorative book about an East African institution is not just a book produced in East Africa — it is a book *for* East African readers, patrons, and stakeholders. Your publishing partner must understand the cultural nuances, distribution landscape, and market dynamics of the region.

This matters when:
- Selecting imagery and narratives that resonate locally
- Navigating ISBN registration, legal deposits, and copyright with Kenyan authorities
- Recommending bookstores and distribution channels that actually reach your audience
- Pricing the book appropriately for the local market while maintaining its premium positioning

**What to ask:** *How do you distribute books in Kenya and across East Africa? What is your experience with institutional and corporate clients in the region?*

GVP has been based in Nairobi since inception. We publish magazines like *Diplomat East Africa* and *Teachers' Image* that are deeply embedded in the East African professional landscape, and our books are stocked in leading bookstores from Nairobi to Kampala to Dar es Salaam.

**Red flag:** The publisher has no physical presence in East Africa and cannot articulate a distribution strategy for the region.

## 4. Transparent, Itemised Pricing

Commemorative book projects represent a significant financial commitment. You deserve to know exactly where your money is going — and to have confidence that the quoted price will be the final price.

A reputable publishing partner provides:
- A detailed quotation breaking down editorial, design, pre-press, printing, and distribution costs
- Clear terms on what is included and what constitutes a change order
- A realistic timeline with milestone-based payment schedules
- Alternative pricing scenarios (premium, standard, and value options)

**What to ask:** *Can you provide a full line-item quotation? What are the most common cost overruns, and how do you prevent them?*

At GVP, our quotations are exhaustive. We also walk clients through cost-saving options such as paper alternatives, print run adjustments, and format changes so you can make informed decisions without surprises.

**Red flag:** Vague estimates, refusal to itemise, or pressure to sign before you fully understand the cost breakdown.

## 5. Complete Project Management

Producing a commemorative book involves dozens of moving parts: manuscript development, photo sourcing, design rounds, proof approvals, colour correction, printing supervision, shipping, customs clearance, and distribution. If you have to coordinate all of these yourself, the project can quickly become overwhelming.

The ideal publishing partner offers a single point of contact and manages the entire production chain.

**What to ask:** *Who is our primary point of contact? How do you handle project communication and milestone tracking? What happens if something goes wrong with the printer?*

At GVP, every project is assigned a dedicated project manager who serves as your single point of contact from brief to delivery. We manage the entire workflow internally and with our trusted network of international printers. When issues arise — and on complex projects they occasionally do — we solve them. You never have to chase a supplier in another time zone.

**Red flag:** The publisher says "we will introduce you to our printer" and expects you to manage the printing relationship yourself. You are paying for management, not introductions.
