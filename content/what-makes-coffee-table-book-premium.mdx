# What Makes a Coffee Table Book Premium?

Walk into any elegant boardroom, hotel lobby, or luxury home in Nairobi, and you'll likely spot one: a large-format book displayed prominently on a table, its cover inviting you to pick it up and leaf through its pages.

Not all coffee table books are created equal, though. Some sit unopened, gathering dust. Others are picked up again and again, their pages worn from repeated enjoyment. What separates the two is rarely the content alone it is the quality of the physical object itself.

At Global Village Publishers, we have spent nearly two decades producing commemorative and premium books for institutions, corporations, and individuals across Kenya and East Africa. We have learned that a truly premium coffee table book is the sum of deliberate choices made at every stage of production. Here is what those choices look like.

## Paper: The Foundation of Feel

The first thing anyone notices when they pick up a premium book is the paper. It is a tactile experience that happens before a single word is read.

**Standard trade publishing** typically uses 80–100 gsm (grams per square metre) wood-free paper. It is functional, affordable, and adequate for novels and text-heavy books. But hold it next to a premium coffee table book, and the difference is immediate.

**Premium coffee table books** use art paper typically 150–200 gsm with a coated or matt finish. The extra weight gives the page a satisfying heft. More importantly, the coating allows for sharp, vibrant reproduction of photographs and artwork. Images do not bleed or appear washed out; they pop.

The choice between gloss and matt art paper is both aesthetic and practical:

- **Gloss art paper** delivers the sharpest, most vivid image reproduction. Colours are punchy, and detail is razor-sharp. It is ideal for photography-heavy books, architectural portfolios, and wildlife collections.
- **Matt art paper** eliminates glare, making it easier to read text-heavy spreads under direct lighting. It offers a more subdued, elegant feel perfect for historical retrospectives and literary coffee table books.
- **Textured or uncoated stock** is sometimes used for vintage or artisanal aesthetics, though it sacrifices some image sharpness for a handcrafted feel.

The rule of thumb: if an image is worth printing large, it deserves paper that does it justice.

## Binding: Built to Last

A premium coffee table book should lie flat when open, withstand repeated handling, and last for decades. That requires binding that goes beyond the bare minimum.

**Case binding** (also called hardcover binding) is the gold standard for premium books. Unlike paperback binding, where the cover is simply glued to the spine, case binding creates a separate cover board that is wrapped in cloth, leather, or paper and then attached to the book block. The result is a book that can endure years of use without its spine cracking or pages falling out.

Within case binding, the quality benchmark is **Smyth-sewn binding**. Here is why it matters:

In standard perfect binding (what most paperbacks use), pages are held together by glue alone. Over time especially in humid climates like Kenya's the glue can dry, crack, or loosen, and pages start to detach.

Smyth-sewn binding physically stitches each signature (group of pages) to the next using reinforced thread. The glue reinforces the stitching; it does not replace it. A Smyth-sewn book can open flat on a table without damaging the spine, and it will outlast glue-only binding by decades.

At GVP, every commemorative book we produce uses Smyth-sewn case binding as standard. It is more expensive typically 30–40 per cent more than perfect binding but for a book meant to last generations, it is non-negotiable.

## Cover Treatments: The Art of First Impressions

If paper is the foundation and binding is the structure, the cover is the promise. It is what draws someone to pick up the book in the first place.

Premium covers go far beyond a printed image wrapped in plastic laminate. Here are the treatments that define the category:

**Foil stamping** uses heat and pressure to apply metallic foil (gold, silver, copper, or custom colours) to the cover. It catches light differently from every angle, adding depth and luxury. Used sparingly for a title, an emblem, or a border it elevates a cover from ordinary to museum quality.

**Embossing** raises selected elements of the cover design above the surface, creating a three-dimensional effect you can feel with your fingertips. Run your hand across the cover of a carefully embossed book, and you will feel the texture before you even open it. **Debossing** creates the opposite effect pressing elements into the cover for a recessed, understated elegance.

**Spot UV** applies a glossy, raised coating to specific areas of a matt cover a title, an image, a logo creating contrast and depth. It is less expensive than foil or embossing but can produce striking results when used thoughtfully.

A truly premium book might combine all three: a foiled and embossed title on a cover bound in cloth, with spot UV on select photographic elements. The layering of textures creates an object that feels valuable from the first touch.

## Photography and Image Resolution

Content is king, but in a coffee table book, images are the crown jewels. Premium books demand images that can stand up to large-format reproduction.

Standard web-resolution images (72 dpi) that look fine on a phone screen will appear pixelated and soft when printed at A4 or larger. Premium books require images at **300 dpi at final printed size** minimum. For full-bleed spreads (images that run across two pages), that means source files in the 20–50 MB range.

Every image that goes into a GVP coffee table book passes through a thorough quality gate: resolution check, colour correction, and proofing against the final paper stock. We have rejected images that were otherwise perfect because they could not survive large-format reproduction. The reader may not know why an image looks crisp, but they will notice immediately if it does not.

## Editorial Rigour: The Invisible Craft

Physical quality matters, but a premium book that is poorly written or factually sloppy is just an expensive disappointment.

Editorial rigour in premium publishing means:

- **Fact-checking every claim** names, dates, titles, historical events
- **Professional copy-editing** that respects the author's voice while ensuring consistency, clarity, and correctness
- **Proofreading** multiple rounds, by different readers, including a final pass on the printed proof
- **Indexing**, where appropriate, to make the book useful as a reference work

In our experience producing the *Best of Kenya* series and institutional commemorative books, the editorial phase typically takes as long as the design and production phases combined. That is not inefficiency it is the difference between a book people browse and a book people treasure.

## Limited Editions and the Scarcity Factor

Many premium coffee table books are published as limited editions numbered copies, often signed by the author or subject, with runs of 500 to 2,500 copies. Once they are gone, they are gone.

Limited runs allow the publisher to invest more per copy in materials and craftsmanship. A print run of 500 Smyth-sewn books, stamped with metallic foil and bound in cloth, costs more per unit than a run of 5,000 glued paperbacks but the result is an object that holds or appreciates in value.

## The Contrast with Standard Trade Publishing

To summarise the difference:

| Element | Standard Trade Book | Premium Coffee Table Book |
|---------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| Paper | 80–100 gsm, uncoated | 150–200 gsm, art/matt |
| Binding | Perfect bound (glue) | Smyth-sewn case bound |
| Cover | Laminated paperback | Cloth/leather, foil, emboss |
| Images | 150–200 dpi, small format | 300 dpi, full bleed |
| Editorial | Single round of proofreading | Multi-stage editing plus fact-check |
| Print run | 1,000–10,000+ | 500–2,000 (often numbered) |
| Longevity | 5–10 years | 50+ years |
