# How to Commission a Commemorative Book: A Practical Guide

A commemorative book is more than ink on paper it is a permanent record of a milestone, a legacy captured for generations. Whether you are celebrating a corporate anniversary, an institutional centenary, a state occasion, or a prominent individual's lifetime of achievement, the process of bringing that vision to life deserves careful planning.

At Global Village Publishers E.A Ltd, we have had the privilege of producing some of East Africa's most cherished commemorative works including the *Best of Kenya* series, institutional histories, and books marking national milestones. Over the years, we have refined a commissioning process that takes a project from concept to finished volume with clarity, efficiency, and excellence.

Here is a practical guide to commissioning a commemorative book, whether you are doing so for the first time or building on previous experience. We have also written about [why institutions invest in these volumes](https://globalvillagepublishers.com) it is worth a read if you are still weighing the decision.

## Step 1: Define Your Rationale and Scope

Every great commemorative book starts with a clear answer to one question: *Why this book, and why now?*

Sit down with your stakeholders and articulate the core purpose. Is this about honouring a founder's legacy? Marking fifty years of institutional impact? Preserving the visual and narrative heritage of a region? The answer shapes every decision that follows tone, content, format, print run, and budget.

At this stage, also define scope. What period does the book cover? What themes must be included? Who are the key contributors or subjects? A clear scope prevents scope creep later, when new ideas inevitably surface.

**GVP tip:** We recommend drafting a single page concept brief that answers the *who, what, why, when, and for whom* of your book. This becomes your north star throughout the project.

## Step 2: Assemble Your Content Team

Commemorative books are collaborative by nature. You will need:

- **An editorial lead** someone who understands narrative flow and can stitch together contributions from multiple writers.
- **Researchers** to verify dates, names, and facts. Accuracy is essential in a book meant to stand as an authoritative record.
- **Contributors** specialists in your field, senior leaders, or community figures who can provide depth and personal perspective.
- **A photo/media curator** someone to source, catalogue, and caption images from archives, private collections, and institutional records.

If your organisation lacks internal editorial capacity, your publishing partner can recommend or supply experienced professionals. At GVP, we maintain a network of editorial specialists familiar with East African institutional and commemorative publishing.

## Step 3: Choose a Publishing Partner

This is arguably the most consequential decision you will make. A publishing partner does far more than print pages they guide you on format, design, paper stock, timeline, and distribution.

Look for a partner with:
- A demonstrable track record in commemorative and coffee table books
- An understanding of the East African market
- Internal design and editorial capability (not just a print shop)
- The ability to advise on ISBN registration, copyright, and legal deposits

## Step 4: Develop the Creative Brief and Book Map

Once your publishing partner is on board, the next phase is creative planning. This involves:

- **Format selection** Will this be a hardcover coffee table book, a slipcased limited edition, or a softcover institutional volume? Coffee table books (typically 250 mm × 250 mm or 280 mm × 240 mm) are the gold standard for commemorative works because of their visual impact.
- **Page count estimate** Based on your content inventory.
- **Design direction** Classic and dignified, or modern and vibrant? The design must match the subject's character.
- **Book map** An outline spread across chapters, approved by all stakeholders.

At GVP, we produce a detailed *book map and sample spread* during this phase a visual prototype of two to four spreads that demonstrates the look and feel before a single page is laid out. This eliminates guesswork and aligns expectations.

## Step 5: Content Development and Curation

Now the real work begins. Your editorial team drafts chapters, your photo curator gathers and selects images, and your publishing partner's designers begin building layouts.

Key milestones in this phase:
- **Manuscript delivery** All text submitted for editing and fact-checking
- **Image selection** Final picks, with captions and credit lines
- **First layout pass** Rough pagination showing text and image placement
- **Editing rounds** Structural edit, copy-edit, and proofread

Timelines vary considerably. A 200 page commemorative book typically requires **4 to 6 months** for content development and design, depending on the volume of archival material and the number of stakeholders involved in approvals.

## Step 6: Design and Preproduction

Design is where a commemorative book becomes an artefact. Professional layout typography, colour management, image resolution, and print specification separates a cherished keepsake from an ordinary publication.

Things that matter at this stage:
- **Image resolution** All images should be at least 300 dpi at the size they appear in print. GVP's preproduction team can advise on scanning resolutions and restoration of older photographs.
- **Colour proofing** A calibrated proof ensures the printed result matches the designer's intent. We produce laminated proofs before the full print run.
- **Paper and binding** The choice of paper (coated art paper, uncoated textured stock, or matte) dramatically affects both feel and cost. Binding options include sewn hardcover, lay-flat binding, and perfect binding.

## Step 7: Printing and Production

When the proofs are signed off, production begins. This is where your publishing partner's relationships with quality printers become critical.

For East African clients, GVP coordinates printing through our network of leading international printers many of our titles are produced in South Korea, China, or Italy on presses known for museum quality colour reproduction. We manage every aspect of print supervision, from colour calibration to binding QA.

A typical print run for a commemorative book ranges from **1,000 to 5,000 copies**, though runs as small as 500 (or as large as 10,000) are possible depending on the brief.

## Step 8: Launch, Distribution, and Legacy

A commemorative book deserves a launch befitting its subject. Whether it is a gala event, a private presentation to dignitaries, or a school ceremony, the launch is the moment your hard work becomes part of the public record.

Beyond the launch, consider:
- **Distribution channels** Bookshops, museum shops, corporate gifting, and direct sales
- **Institutional deposits** Kenya requires copies to be lodged with the Kenya National Library Service and the Library of Congress
- **Digital legacy** An e-book version or a companion microsite extends the book's reach
