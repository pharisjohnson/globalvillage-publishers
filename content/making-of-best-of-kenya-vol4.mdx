# The Making of Best of Kenya Vol. 4: The Inside Story of East Africa's Premier Coffee Table Book

When the first *Best of Kenya* volume appeared on Nairobi's bookshelves, it filled a gap that few had even articulated. Here was a book that captured Kenya not as a tourist brochure or a statistical digest, but as a lived experience a mosaic of landscapes, faces, achievements, and moments that together told the story of a nation.

By the time we began production on *Best of Kenya Vol. 4*, the series had become the definitive visual chronicle of contemporary Kenya. The pressure was on. And the journey, as always, was extraordinary.

Here is a look at how Global Village Publishers E.A Ltd brought *Best of Kenya Vol. 4* from concept to coffee table.

## The Seed of an Idea

The *Best of Kenya* series began with a simple premise: Kenya deserves a book that does justice to its diversity, beauty, and achievement not through the lens of a foreign publisher or a tourism campaign, but through the eyes of Kenyans themselves.

By Vol. 4, that premise had evolved. The world had changed. Kenya had changed. The fourth volume needed to reflect a nation that was increasingly digital, increasingly youthful, and increasingly confident on the global stage. It needed to honour the timeless landscapes the savannahs, the mountains, the wildlife while also documenting what was new and emergent.

The editorial team at GVP spent three months in the research and concept phase alone: reviewing hundreds of articles, consulting with cultural advisors across Kenya's 47 counties, and mapping the thematic spine of the volume.

## Building the Content Architecture

Every *Best of Kenya* volume is structured around pillars that define national life. For Vol. 4, the pillars were:

1. **The Land** Kenya's geography, from the Indian Ocean coastline to the Rift Valley highlands and the northern deserts
2. **The People** Portraits of communities, innovators, artisans, and everyday Kenyans
3. **Culture & Heritage** Music, literature, fashion, cuisine, and the preservation of indigenous traditions
4. **Enterprise & Innovation** Kenya's tech ecosystem, manufacturing renaissance, and entrepreneurial spirit
5. **Conservation & Sustainability** The guardians of Kenya's natural heritage
6. **Sport & Achievement** From marathon champions to emerging sporting frontiers

Each pillar was assigned a lead editor and a visual curator. The brief was not to be exhaustive but to be *representative*. Every spread had to earn its place.

## The Visual Journey

Coffee table books live or die on their imagery, and *Best of Kenya Vol. 4* raised the bar higher than any previous volume.

Our photo team reviewed over **15,000 images** submissions from professional photographers, institutional archives, community collections, and commissioned shoots. From these, approximately **280** made the final cut.

The selection process was rigorous. Every candidate image was evaluated on four criteria:
- **Emotional resonance** Does it make you feel something?
- **Technical quality** Does it meet print-grade resolution and composition standards?
- **Authenticity** Does it represent Kenya honestly, without cliché or stereotype?
- **Narrative contribution** Does it advance the story the spread is telling?

One of the most rewarding aspects of Vol. 4 was the inclusion of photographs from emerging Kenyan photographers alongside established names. Discovering new talent and giving it a national platform is a tradition the series is proud to continue.

> *"The image that stayed with me longest was a dusk photograph of a young Maasai woman in Nairobi's Kilimani area phone in one hand, traditional shuka draped over her shoulder. It captured the duality of modern Kenya better than any paragraph could."* GVP Senior Photo Editor

## Design: Where Craft Meets Art

The design of a coffee table book is invisible when it is done well and glaring when it is not. The reader should feel the quality without thinking about it.

For Vol. 4, GVP's design team made several deliberate choices:

**A new colour palette.** Each chapter opened with a bold full-bleed spread anchored by a dominant colour drawn from the Kenyan landscape ochre for the Land chapter, indigo for the People chapter, gold for Enterprise.

**Typography.** We selected a typeface that balanced readability with personality. The body text needed to work at the sizes coffee table readers expect generous but not sprawling while the headline type needed to command the page.

**Paper and finish.** The volume was printed on 170 gsm matt art paper with a sewn hardcover binding chosen for durability, colour reproduction, and the tactile quality that distinguishes a premium book. The cover features lettering stamped with metallic foil and a spot UV photograph that catches the light.

**The Kenya-first approach.** Every element from the endpapers (a map of Kenya illustrated by a Nairobi-based artist) to the chapter dividers (patterned with motifs from Kenyan crafts) was sourced from Kenyan creative talent.

## The Production Marathon

With the design finalised, we moved into pre-production the phase where art meets engineering.

Each spread was colour-corrected to ensure that the printed result matched the designer's intent. This is not automatic; different paper stocks absorb ink differently, and what looks perfect on a calibrated monitor can appear flat or oversaturated on the page. Our pre-press team created press-ready files with meticulous attention to colour profiles.

Printing was coordinated with our long-standing partner in South Korea, whose presses handle the museum-grade colour reproduction the *Best of Kenya* series demands. A GVP production manager was on-site for the press run, approving each signature as it came off the line.

Shipping, customs clearance, and warehousing were managed through our Nairobi logistics hub. From the time the first page was printed to the day the first copy arrived at a Nairobi bookshop, the entire production chain was tracked and managed by the GVP team.

## The Launch

*Best of Kenya Vol. 4* launched at a Nairobi venue that reflected the book's theme a celebration of Kenyan achievement attended by contributors, photographers, cultural leaders, and the readers who had made the series what it is.

The response exceeded even our expectations. Within the first month, the initial print run was nearly sold out, with orders coming not just from Kenya but from diaspora communities in the UK, the US, and the UAE.
