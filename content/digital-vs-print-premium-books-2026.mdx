# Digital vs. Print: Why Premium Books Still Matter in 2026

It's a question we hear almost weekly: "Does anyone still buy physical books? Isn't everything digital now?"

It's a fair question. We live in an age where you can carry thousands of books on a device that fits in your pocket. PDFs fly across WhatsApp groups. Ebooks are convenient, searchable, and never go out of stock. With social media eating up more of our attention every year, you'd be forgiven for thinking the printed page has had its day.

But here's the thing: premium print isn't just surviving — it's experiencing a quiet renaissance. And nowhere is that more visible than right here in Kenya.

## The screen fatigue is real

Let's start with what everyone feels but few people name: screen fatigue. If you're reading this on a phone or laptop, you know exactly what I'm talking about. The eye strain. The endless scrolling. The way notifications yank your attention every thirty seconds.

After a full day staring at screens — Zoom calls, emails, spreadsheets, Instagram — the last thing many people want is to stare at *another* screen for pleasure or learning. A physical book offers something increasingly precious: a break. No backlight. No notifications. No temptation to swipe over to Twitter.

This isn't just anecdotal. Global print book sales have held remarkably steady over the past decade, even as ebooks have plateaued. In markets like the US and UK, hardcover sales have actually *grown* in recent years. The pattern in Kenya mirrors this: there's a growing appetite for well-made physical books that you can hold, display, and pass on.

But here's where we need to draw an important distinction. We're not talking about mass-market paperbacks or throwaway pamphlets. We're talking about *premium* books — the kind you invest in intentionally.

## The permanence problem with digital

Here's a thought experiment. Think about the most important digital document you created ten years ago. Can you still open it? Is the format still supported? Is it on a hard drive you still own, stored in a cloud service that still exists?

Digital formats have a shelf life problem. File formats become obsolete. Hard drives fail. Cloud services shut down. Even something as simple as a PDF can be lost when the only copy sits on a laptop that gets replaced every few years.

Now think about a commemorative book your school published for its 50th anniversary in 1995. That book is probably still sitting in the school library, on alumni shelves, in community centres. It doesn't need a password. It doesn't need a compatible device. It doesn't need an internet connection. It just *exists*, as solid and readable today as the day it was printed.

This is why we say at Global Village Publishers that a premium book isn't just a publication — it's a time capsule. When Starehe Boys' Centre or Strathmore University commissions a commemorative volume, they're not just creating a product for today. They're creating an artefact that will tell their story to students, staff, and visitors fifty years from now.

Digital is instant. Print is permanent.

## The status signal of a premium book

Let's be honest about something that publishing professionals rarely say out loud: a beautiful book on a coffee table sends a signal.

When you walk into a CEO's office and see "Best of Kenya" on the table, or a school's centenary book in the reception area, it communicates something. It says: *We have a story worth telling. We have a legacy worth preserving. We invest in quality.*

In Kenya's business and institutional landscape, where reputation and relationships matter enormously, premium print remains a powerful credibility tool. A corporate history book given to clients and partners at a milestone anniversary does something a PDF attachment never could: it shows you cared enough to produce something enduring.

Think about it this way. When was the last time someone proudly showed you a PDF they received as a gift? Conversely, when was the last time you saw someone display a beautifully bound book on their shelf and felt a twinge of admiration?

The Kenyan market understands this instinctively. Our work with institutions across the country — from corporates marking anniversaries to schools celebrating centenaries — consistently shows that decision-makers value the tangible over the ephemeral. They understand that a premium book isn't just content wrapped in cardboard. It's a statement.

## Tactility changes everything

There's a reason coffee table books exist as a category. It's not about the words — it's about the *experience*.

A premium book engages more than your eyes. The weight of quality paper. The texture of a cloth-bound cover. The subtle sheen of a foil-stamped title. The way light plays across a full-bleed photograph on matte art paper. These aren't decorative details — they're the entire point.

When we produce a book at Global Village Publishers, every material choice matters. The paper stock affects how colours render. The binding method determines how the book feels when you open it. The cover finish — matte or gloss, with or without foil — shapes the first impression before anyone reads a single word.

These sensory elements create emotional resonance. They're why people pick up a premium book and instinctively say "wow." And they're why, even in 2026, there's no digital substitute for holding something beautifully made in your hands.

## The new appreciation for physical things

Here's something interesting: the generation that grew up fully digital — Gen Z and younger millennials — is driving a resurgence in physical formats. Vinyl records. Film cameras. Physical books.

Why? Because when you've grown up with unlimited access to everything digital, physical objects become *special*. They're curated. They're intentional. You can't just download a premium book — you have to choose it, acquire it, and make space for it in your life. That very friction is what gives it value.

In Nairobi, we've seen young professionals and entrepreneurs increasingly invest in premium books for their homes and offices. They want something that reflects their taste, their achievements, and their story. A PDF on a tablet doesn't do that. A beautifully bound commemorative book on a shelf absolutely does.

## Where Kenya fits in

Kenya's publishing landscape is unique. We have a vibrant oral tradition. We value storytelling. We celebrate milestones with ceremony and pride. These cultural instincts align perfectly with premium print.

When a Kenyan school celebrates 100 years, a commemorative book isn't just a nice-to-have. It's a way of saying: *This institution matters. The generations who built it matter. The students who will pass through its gates matter.*

When a Kenyan company reaches 25 or 50 years, a corporate history book captures not just the financial milestones but the people, the challenges, the character that got them there.

This is why premium print isn't going anywhere. It meets a deep human need that digital can't touch: the need to hold our stories in our hands and pass them to the people who come after us.

## The bottom line

So, does print still matter in 2026? Yes — more than ever, in fact. But not the kind of print that's trying to compete with digital on convenience, speed, or cost. That's a losing battle.

Premium print wins on a different playing field entirely. It competes on permanence, on beauty, on emotional resonance, on the sheer power of a physical object that tells the world: *This matters*.

At Global Village Publishers, we've been making books worth keeping since 2008. We've seen trends come and go. And through it all, the satisfaction on a client's face when they hold their finished book for the first time has never changed.

Digital is for consuming. Premium print is for *keeping*.

And in 2026, that distinction matters more than ever.
