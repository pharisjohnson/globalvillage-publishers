# Teachers' Image: 35 Volumes of Celebrating Kenyan Educators

Every teacher remembers the moment. That handwritten note from a student who finally understood a difficult concept. The colleague who went beyond the call of duty to cover a class. The head teacher who stayed late to counsel a troubled learner.

In Kenya, these moments happen every day in city classrooms, rural primary schools, and everything in between. But for too long, they went largely unrecognised beyond the school gate.

That is where *Teachers' Image* magazine comes in.

## The Magazine That Puts Teachers First

When Global Village Publishers launched *Teachers' Image* magazine, the vision was clear: create a dedicated platform that celebrates Kenyan educators not just as professionals delivering the curriculum, but as shapers of character, community pillars, and nation-builders.

What started as a bold idea has now reached an extraordinary milestone: **35 volumes** of continuous publication. For a specialised magazine in the Kenyan market, this is no small feat. It represents nearly a decade of unwavering commitment to telling the stories of the men and women who educate our nation's children.

Each volume typically spanning six months of content captures the heartbeat of Kenyan education. From the teacher in Turkana who walks 10 kilometres to school every morning to the innovative educator in Nairobi using gamification to teach mathematics, every story matters.

## How Stories Are Sourced

One question we hear often is: *How do you find these teachers?*

The answer is a combination of deliberate outreach, reader nominations, and partnerships with the Teachers Service Commission (TSC). Schools and communities nominate educators who have made a tangible difference. TSC regional offices flag exceptional performance. Parents write in. Former students send heartfelt tributes.

Our editorial team then works to verify and develop each story conducting interviews, visiting schools where possible, and capturing the photographs that bring each narrative to life. The result is authentic journalism that respects the dignity of the teaching profession.

The magazine also features practical content: lesson plans that work, classroom management tips, updates on curriculum changes, and professional development opportunities. It is designed to be both inspirational and immediately useful a resource that a teacher can read during a break and apply the next day.

## The 35th Volume: A Milestone Worth Celebrating

The 35th volume, released in the first half of 2026, is our most ambitious yet. It features:

- **Profiles of 50 exceptional educators** from all 47 counties, selected through a rigorous nomination and verification process
- **A special feature on Kenya's CBC champions** teachers who have embraced the Competency-Based Curriculum with creativity and dedication
- **Historical retrospectives** comparing Kenya's education system across decades, highlighting how the teaching profession has evolved
- **Mental health and wellness resources** for educators, addressing a topic that the profession has too long overlooked

The cover story profiles Jane Wanjiku, a primary school teacher from Kiambu County who developed a peer-mentoring programme that reduced dropout rates among girls in her sub-county by 40 per cent. Her story like every story in *Teachers' Image* reminds us that the most impactful educational reforms often happen not in ministry boardrooms but in classrooms, led by teachers who refuse to accept the status quo.

## Stories That Stay With You

Over 35 volumes, certain stories have become emblematic of what *Teachers' Image* stands for.

There is the story of Joseph Ole Sankori, a secondary school teacher in Kajiado who used his own savings to start a library for his community because the nearest public library was 60 kilometres away. After his profile appeared in Volume 18, a local benefactor donated 2,000 books, and the TSC recognised him with a national teaching award.

There is Sister Grace Akinyi, who has taught at a special needs school in Kisumu for 32 years. Her story in Volume 22 led to a partnership with a Dutch NGO that equipped the school with assistive learning technology.

And there is the tale of the "Maths Bus" a mobile classroom started by three mathematics teachers in Mombasa, featured in Volume 29, which has since reached over 5,000 students across the coastal region.

These stories do more than inspire. They create networks of support, attract resources to deserving schools, and most importantly, they tell teachers: *You are seen. You matter.*

## Impact on Teacher Morale

It is easy to measure a magazine's success by circulation numbers and advertising revenue. But for *Teachers' Image*, the most meaningful metric is harder to quantify but impossible to ignore: morale.

Kenyan teachers face immense challenges. Large class sizes, limited resources, delayed promotions, and sometimes hostile public perception. In this context, recognition is not a luxury. It is a necessity.

Research in organisational psychology consistently shows that recognition is one of the most powerful drivers of workplace satisfaction and performance. For teachers, who rarely receive the public acknowledgment that other professions take for granted, a feature in *Teachers' Image* can be transformative.

We have heard from head teachers who frame magazine pages and display them in staff rooms. From TSC officials who reference profiles during teacher award ceremonies. From young people who decided to pursue teaching after reading about an educator who changed lives.

One reader, a teacher from Nakuru, wrote to us after Volume 30: *"For years, I felt invisible. This magazine made me remember why I chose this profession."*

That is impact you cannot capture in a media kit.

## What Is Next

As *Teachers' Image* moves beyond 35 volumes, the vision is expanding. We are developing a digital companion platform where educators can share resources, nominate colleagues in real time, and access a searchable archive of every profile published. We are partnering with additional teacher training institutions to distribute the magazine directly to student teachers. And we are exploring regional editions to cover educators in Uganda, Tanzania, and Rwanda.

The magazine remains free to all Kenyan teachers a commitment we have maintained since Volume 1 and one we intend to keep. Advertising and institutional partnerships fund the production, ensuring that the teachers themselves never bear the cost of being celebrated.
